## Pitch Truth

One-line premise:

> A cash-rich mid-sized group heir, treated as a decorative son, touches dying ledgers and sees the hidden cashflows, recoverable rights, and 90-day survival windows inside distressed assets; he buys what everyone else calls trash and turns it into control, cash, and family power.

Reader promise:

- The reader watches a self-interested operator turn ugly assets into visible money.
- Each win must prove three things in-scene: the protagonist saw a concealed value gap, acquired control cheaply, and converted that control into cashflow or authority.
- The protagonist does not need to be kind or cruel. His default filter is profit, efficiency, risk defense, and position improvement.

Why now:

- Korean webnovel platforms continue to reward titles with immediate ability/reward clarity.
- Current real-world conversation around PF, NPL, workout, restructuring, logistics cost pressure, and small-business closures gives the premise contemporary texture.
- The professional-money lane benefits from concrete documents, contracts, settlement tables, leases, and debt schedules because they make "numbers fantasy" legible.

Protagonist:

- Name: 한도윤
- Age: 32
- Position: only son of `한성푸드로지스`, a cash-rich mid-sized food/logistics group.
- Public label: "엑셀 배운 도련님."
- Real competence: former private-equity restructuring analyst who can read broken businesses as bundles of rights, bottlenecks, and cashflow claims.
- Supernatural edge: by touching real ledgers, card-settlement tables, lease files, logistics rate sheets, debt schedules, or vendor contracts, he sees hidden cashflow leakage, recoverable rights, fraud residue, bottleneck costs, and an approximate 90-day survival path.

Ability constraints:

- Requires actual documents or operating data.
- Does not read minds.
- Does not predict stock, coin, lottery, or macro prices.
- The visible number is not free money. It becomes real only if he wins authority, fixes operations, and survives counter-moves.
- The better the target's records, the sharper the signal. Rotten records produce partial and dangerous readings.

Core engine:

1. Find a distressed asset everyone has emotionally written off.
2. Touch the operating documents and identify a hidden recoverable value gap.
3. Buy rights instead of buying the whole burden.
4. Fix one bottleneck faster than incumbents expect.
5. Force public receipt: cashflow, contract, option, priority right, settlement, board seat, or market reevaluation.
6. Use the receipt to access a larger distressed deal.

First deal:

- Target: failing gukbap franchise with 12 stores.
- Wrong market reading: "old founder, tired stores, delivery complaints, overdue rent, no upside."
- Do-yoon's reading: the stores are bad, but the settlement rights, logistics route, trademark-use claim, lease-priority position, group purchasing angle, and debt split are alive.
- First acquisition principle: buy the rights that produce control, not the visible dead storefronts.

Season 1 arc bank:

- Arc 1: failing franchise rights split.
- Arc 2: cold-chain logistics NPL route capture.
- Arc 3: institutional meal supply contract rescue.
- Arc 4: regional commercial district REIT foothold.
- Arc 5: PEF portfolio fight against a senior operator.
- Arc 6: family breach-of-duty frame and boardroom counterattack.
- Arc 7: independent distressed-asset manager launch.

TR pacing contract for this work:

- One TR block is a planning bundle that unfolds into roughly 2-6 episodes.
- Every TR block must contain at least two distinct incident beats:
  - one primary deal/control incident, and
  - one secondary complication, observer shift, counter-move, or next-asset gate.
- A block cannot be only "discover problem -> think about problem."
- Every block must close with a receipt or a sharper danger that directly changes the protagonist's leverage.
- The protagonist's choice logic must remain self-interested: profit, efficiency, survival, retaliation, defense, or position gain.

Contamination guard:

- Do not turn Do-yoon into a charity founder.
- Do not let stock-prophecy, coin-prophecy, lottery, or generic regression replace operational deal-making.
- Do not import donor names, scene order, exact contract surfaces, or proper nouns.
- Do not let family approval become the main reward. Family recognition is a byproduct of control.

## Early Conversion

Opening spike:

- At a family dinner, Do-yoon is dismissed as the decorative only son who failed to become a real executive.
- A group affiliate's dying gukbap franchise is mentioned as a disposal headache.
- Do-yoon touches the franchise ledger and sees a number nobody else sees: the stores are not worth saving, but the rights around them can produce immediate control and cash.

First three-episode conversion:

- Episode 1: insult, disposal meeting, ledger touch, hidden cashflow reveal.
- Episode 2: Do-yoon refuses the full acquisition trap and proposes buying only settlement/logistics/trademark/lease-position rights.
- Episode 3: incumbent manager tries to dump liabilities on him; Do-yoon uses the documents to split the deal and wins the first cheap control position.

First block receipt:

- He extracts a right bundle for a fraction of the apparent business value.
- A doubter sees that he avoided the debt trap.
- A counterparty realizes too late that the "worthless" ledger contained a recoverable cashflow claim.

Secondary incident requirement:

- The opening block must not stop at acquisition.
- It also needs either a leaseholder counter-move, a logistics bottleneck crisis, a founder's hidden side contract, or an observer's reevaluation scene before the block closes.
