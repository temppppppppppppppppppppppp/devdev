## 2. Should We Go To 70 Blocks At Once?

No, not as production TR.

A 70-block one-shot is dangerous because one TR block represents a planning bundle of roughly 2-6 published episodes. That means 70 blocks can imply a long serial range, not a small outline.

Going straight to 70 risks:

- broad sector list replacing scene pressure;
- repeated boardroom/account/quote surfaces;
- money number escalation replacing authority texture;
- identical reward shape every sector;
- S4 receiving labels instead of rooms and people;
- donor lineage turning into surface imitation because there is not enough authored middle tissue.

Allowed:

- a very thin 70-slot macro inventory after full Phase0, clearly labeled as a map and not production TR;
- 10-block TR tranche generation after opening canary proof;
- block-by-block or tranche-by-tranche audit against scene-native pacing.

Not allowed:

- 70 production TR blocks in one pass from the current partial material;
- full BI from selection-ready pitch alone;
- calling the current partial opening Phase0 a complete Phase0.

## 5. Real Manuscript Pacing Standard

Real successful manuscripts do not usually pause to explain the whole machine before the reader gets paid.

For this work, the opening must feel like:

- episode 1: return rupture and immediate current-room problem;
- episode 1~2: file/protocol proof before family lecture;
- episode 2: conditional account/access reward;
- episode 2~3: helper by contract;
- episode 3: external price/tradability proof;
- episode 4~5: money converts to team loyalty and audit access.

If the opening spends 5 episodes on:

- family meeting exposition;
- old-life summary;
- market lecture;
- "I know the future" explanation;
- investment preparation without visible receipt;

then it is not following the successful-manuscript pacing we are trying to learn.
