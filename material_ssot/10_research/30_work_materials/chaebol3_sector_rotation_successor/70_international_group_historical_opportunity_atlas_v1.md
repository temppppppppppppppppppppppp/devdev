## 0. Purpose

This atlas records the new direction:

Use an International Group-like collapse as the historical reference. The grandfather made a fatal group judgment, the father died long ago in an accident, and the protagonist is the grandson who expected to live as successor but watched the group collapse, died in regret/rage, and returned.

From there, the story grows the group from Korean chaebol to world-top group through historical sector windows and relationships with Korean conglomerate heads and global figures such as Steve Jobs, Masayoshi Son, Elon Musk, Apple, SoftBank, Tesla, and adjacent industries.

This is not a final Phase0.

The existing EP001-EP003 canary remains useful as an opening-engine test.

If the canary passes, the full planning should restart from this atlas.

## 1. Key Structural Decision

For an International Group-style reference, the return point should move earlier than the current 2008 draft.

Best candidate:

- `1984 Q4 ~ 1985 Q1`

Why:

- The International Group collapse/disbandment reference sits in 1985.
- A pre-collapse return lets the protagonist prevent or survive the fatal family/group decision.
- It creates a long runway for 1988 Olympics consumer brands, 1990s semiconductors/CDMA/internet, 1997 IMF distressed acquisitions, 2000 SoftBank/Alibaba, 2002 PayPal/SpaceX, 2004 Tesla, 2007 iPhone, and 2010s battery/data-center/AI.

If the protagonist is 17-19 in 1985:

- 1997: 29-31, credible as a young group operator during IMF.
- 2000: 32-34, credible as a global tech investor.
- 2004: 36-38, credible as a serious counterparty to Tesla/SpaceX/Apple supply chain.
- 2007+: 39+, credible as Korean/global chaebol head.

## 2. International Group Reference Facts

Use facts as reference pressure, not literal legal replay.

Source-backed facts to preserve as inspiration:

- Kukje/International Group was a major Korean conglomerate before its 1985 dissolution/disbandment.
- Contemporary and later reports describe the dissolution as politically driven and involving government/financial authority pressure.
- The Constitutional Court later found the 1985 disbandment unconstitutional, but the group could not simply be restored.
- The group had fiction-useful surfaces: footwear/consumer brand, export, trading, shipping/logistics, finance/banking ties, steel/textile-type industrial assets.

Fictional translation:

- Grandfather's wrong judgment is not merely "bad investment."
- It combines political misread, creditor-bank dependence, export/FX pressure, overextended affiliates, and failure to protect legal/financial proof surfaces.

Opening emotional engine:

- The protagonist did not ruin the group.
- He inherited the shame, debt, and dispossession.
- His rage is not "I should have invested earlier" but "the family let the group die without knowing which doors mattered."

## 3. Revised Core Premise Candidate

> The grandson of a Korean conglomerate ruined by his grandfather's fatal misread returns to the eve of the group's 1985 collapse. His father has long been dead from an accident, and he once expected to inherit a name that vanished. This time he saves the group's first door, then uses every historical sector window from Seoul Olympics consumer brands and Korean CDMA to IMF distressed assets, Apple, SoftBank, Alibaba, PayPal, SpaceX, Tesla, batteries, data centers, and AI infrastructure to turn a fallen Korean chaebol into the world's strongest industrial-investment group.

Commercial promise:

- revenge against collapse;
- restoration of family name;
- money as world-access;
- Korean group becoming global kingmaker;
- encounters with real-world giants through fictionalized deal surfaces.

## 5. Real-Person Use Rules

Use real people as:

- meeting gate;
- market symbol;
- investment window;
- negotiation pressure;
- public historical anchor.

Avoid:

- defamatory claims;
- invented private criminal behavior;
- making real people act like puppets;
- protagonist "teaching" Steve Jobs or Elon Musk how to build their own companies;
- direct quote fabrication presented as real.

Best fiction posture:

- The protagonist earns a seat by solving a concrete supply, capital, or market problem.
- Real figures remain formidable.
- The pleasure is not "I lecture Jobs/Musk/Son"; it is "I prepared the only Korean industrial door they need."
