## 0. Purpose

This file upgrades the International Group direction from a historical opportunity map into an opening-engine seed.

Quality bar:

- The first 1-3 episodes must be writable as scenes, not as a history report.
- The protagonist must act through one narrow document/object first.
- The first reward must be a file/route/access movement before profit.
- The grandfather's wrong judgment must appear as a current-room decision, not as a villain essay.

## 1. Current Best Opening Choice

Best first gate:

`creditor-bank pressure + export LC + Busan supplier/payroll damage + 72-hour hold`

Why this is stronger than a generic investment opening:

- It makes the group collapse personal and material from page one.
- It gives the protagonist a small, believable lever: one document, one deadline, one hold.
- It lets adults resist rationally because they own bank exposure, payroll, political pressure, and delivery failure.
- It creates the first reward as access: the protagonist gets into the crisis file path.
- It naturally opens Arc 02: the saved footwear/export affiliate becomes the sportswear/consumer-brand revival door.

Avoid first gate:

- stock purchase;
- abstract political conspiracy;
- family dining-room accusation;
- global future-history lecture;
- famous-person cameo.

Hard correction after 3-pass audit:

- The LC does not save the group. It only buys time.
- Exact first held action: `emergency affiliate sale / breakup action is held for 72 hours while foreign buyer continuity and supplier-payroll exposure are confirmed`.
- Bank officer's risk: if he stamps the breakup before the buyer telex and payroll exposure are checked, his branch/desk can be blamed for converting a recoverable export problem into a larger loan-loss, local-payroll, and foreign-buyer failure. The fear is not pure legal liability; it is career risk, exposure risk, and superior-blame risk.
- Protagonist expertise source: failed-future liquidation records, lawsuit exhibits, auction notices, and creditor memos. He recognizes the paper route because he lived through the postmortem, not because he is a teenage business genius.
- Minimum EP001 visible receipt: `memo moved -> chair assigned -> telex access -> named adult asks who wrote it`.

## 4. Grandfather's Fatal Judgment

The grandfather's mistake should be narrow enough to dramatize.

Not:

- "He was stupid."
- "He offended the regime."
- "He invested badly."

Better:

1. He believes the main bank will keep honoring checks because the group is too locally important to break.
2. He agrees to sacrifice one footwear/export affiliate to protect the holding core.
3. He misses that the affiliate's export LC, supplier payments, and guarantee chain make the sale trigger wider default.
4. He treats political appeasement as the main problem while the protagonist sees the paper route that can buy 72 hours.

Scene version:

- Grandfather orders the emergency sale folder sealed.
- An uncle pushes the sale because it saves his construction affiliate.
- The bank officer wants a clean breakup because delay makes him responsible.
- The protagonist notices the LC and shipping schedule attached to the "small" affiliate.
- His first ask is not "trust me." It is: "Stamp the sale after this telex is confirmed. If the buyer cancels, the bank owns the payroll freeze."

## 5. Episode 1-3 Seed

### EP001: The Paper That Should Not Be Underneath

Opening body:

- Old-life flash is short: an auction sticker on the last shoe-store sign, a port warehouse shutter, a legal notice with the grandfather's name, and the protagonist's anger that no one knew which document killed the group.
- He dies or blacks out in a place tied to the vanished name, preferably near a warehouse/auction/legal-storage surface.
- He wakes in 1984/1985 with sensory dislocation: rubber smell, carbon-copy ink, rotary phone bell, wrist/date mismatch, young hand.

Job-frame clarity:

- He is not lounging as a rich kid.
- He is a fatherless direct-line grandson assigned to carry folders and observe the export/finance corridor as "successor training" no one takes seriously.
- His current task: deliver an emergency folder from export division to the chairman-side secretary before the bank meeting.

First object:

- emergency asset-sale folder;
- attached export LC confirmation request;
- shipping manifest for footwear/rubber goods;
- Busan supplier payroll note;
- bank line freeze telex.

First action:

- He does not explain the future.
- He pulls the LC sheet from the bottom, circles the loading deadline, and writes a one-page memo:
  - selling the affiliate first cancels the buyer route;
  - cancellation freezes supplier payroll;
  - payroll freeze becomes port/local-political cost;
  - bank should prefer a 72-hour hold over immediate breakup.
- He does not claim the LC solves the crisis. His memo only argues that immediate stamping creates a larger bank-owned cost than a short hold.

First adult movement:

- The secretary initially treats him as an errand boy.
- After seeing the LC number and buyer deadline, she changes the folder route.
- The memo moves from "family copy" to the bank-meeting folder.

EP001 receipt:

- The protagonist does not win the group.
- He gets one chair outside the creditor-bank meeting and permission to stay by the telex phone.
- The last beat is the bank officer asking, "Who wrote this line?"
- The group remains in danger. The receipt is movement of procedure, not rescue.

### EP002: The Hold Is Not Trust

Opening pressure:

- Bank officer wants clean asset sale.
- Uncle wants the footwear/export affiliate sold because it protects his own affiliate.
- Grandfather is furious that a grandson touched a sealed folder.

Protagonist lever:

- He asks one narrow question: if the foreign buyer cancels after the bank pushes the sale, who carries the supplier/payroll/public failure?
- He requests a 72-hour hold and one outgoing telex to confirm buyer intent.
- He frames the hold as a risk-control action for the bank officer, not a favor to the family.

Resistance:

- Bank officer says delay makes him responsible.
- Grandfather says children do not negotiate bank rooms.
- Uncle says the boy is hiding behind his dead father's name.

Protagonist response:

- No shouting.
- He accepts liability boundary: no public claim, no authority title, only a written "export recovery memo" under the secretary/legal officer's name.

EP002 receipt:

- 72-hour hold.
- one telex room access window;
- two staff assigned under supervision;
- grandfather's condition: if the telex fails, the protagonist loses all corridor access.
- bank officer's condition: the memo must remain an internal export recovery note, not a public allegation.

### EP003: Busan Answers Back

Scene move:

- Go out of chairman-room gravity.
- Use Busan export desk, port schedule, supplier payroll, or buyer telex.

Human cost:

- A supplier representative has payroll due Friday.
- A shipping clerk has containers waiting.
- A factory manager knows one missed shipment kills the buyer relationship.

Protagonist lever:

- He does not "save everyone."
- He gets the foreign buyer to confirm that shipment continuity, not ownership structure, is the condition.
- He uses that to make the bank's immediate-sale plan look more expensive than the 72-hour hold.

EP003 receipt:

- buyer telex becomes external witness;
- supplier/payroll route gets a partial release;
- grandfather signs a conditional crisis desk memo;
- the saved footwear/export affiliate becomes Arc 02's consumer-brand/sportswear door.

## 9. Forbidden AI-Slop

Do not write these as opening logic:

- "나는 국제그룹이 왜 망했는지 알고 있었다."
- "이번 생에는 세계 최고의 그룹을 만들 것이다."
- "역사는 곧 나의 무기였다."
- "모든 것은 서류 한 장에서 시작됐다" followed by no actual document behavior.
- "할아버지는 틀렸고 나는 옳았다" before a room changes.
- "일론 머스크, 스티브 잡스, 손정의가 내 미래였다" in the opening.

Replace with:

- a sheet pulled from the wrong folder;
- a number circled;
- an adult pausing before stamping;
- a telex request allowed for 72 hours;
- one person whose payroll or shipment changes.
